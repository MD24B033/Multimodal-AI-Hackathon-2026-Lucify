[00:00.00 – 00:36.63]  Diagram — architecture
Lucify is a source-grounded study companion. I built it as a single FastAPI service with four pipelines — ingestion, grounded tutoring, adaptive assessment, and a learner model — all sharing one Postgres database with pgvector. The frontend is Next.js and React. Upstash Redis carries live progress and a token budget shared across processes. Language models do only the work that genuinely needs them. Retrieval and every learner calculation run locally, and that is what keeps the whole system inside a free tier.

[00:36.63 – 00:58.86]  Demo clip — access and shell
There is no sign-up. Access is by pre-shared key, one per person. The app is deployed on free Railway and Neon tiers, so I wanted a hard bound on who can spend those resources — and a study companion holding someone's coursework should not be open to the internet anyway. Each key maps to one learner, with their own courses and their own progress.

[00:58.86 – 01:33.83]  Diagram — ingestion
Ingestion is where the knowledge base is built. Docling extracts structured text, tables and layout from documents. A vision model reads slides that are images with no text layer, and describes diagrams so they become searchable. FFmpeg and Whisper turn a lecture recording into a timestamped transcript. The detail that matters most is that every unit keeps its origin — a page, a slide number, a bounding box, or a timestamp. Embeddings are then generated locally, which costs no tokens at all.

[01:33.83 – 02:01.36]  Demo clip — ingest live
Here a course is created and a document ingested from scratch. Progress is live, pushed through Redis rather than polled, so the stage and the percentage update as the work happens. This part is sped up.

[02:01.36 – 02:41.52]  Diagram — grounding
Grounded tutoring is the heart of it, and it is built around retrieval rather than prompting. A question is searched two ways at once: semantic search over pgvector, and lexical search through Postgres full-text. Those two ranked lists are combined with Reciprocal Rank Fusion. Then a coverage gate checks whether the retrieved evidence is strong enough, against a hand-tuned threshold of nought point three eight. Below it, the question is refused before the model is ever called. Above it, the model must cite the excerpts it was given, and I validate every citation marker afterwards, dropping any the model invented.

[02:41.52 – 03:16.85]  Demo clip — grounded answer
The answer comes back marked grounded, with numbered citations. Opening one does not just name the source — it opens the document at the cited lines, highlighted on the page.

[03:16.85 – 03:32.88]  Demo clip — refusal
And a question the material does not cover is declined rather than guessed. That refusal is the feature, not a failure — it is what source grounding actually means.

[03:32.88 – 04:09.58]  Diagram — learner
Behind the tutoring sits a learner model. Bayesian Knowledge Tracing estimates the probability a topic is actually known, conditioned on question difficulty. Item Response Theory estimates ability and picks what difficulty to ask next. A forgetting curve and SM-2 scheduling decide when something should be seen again, and prerequisites derived from the material point at foundational gaps. None of this calls a language model. It is closed-form mathematics over the answers already recorded, so it stays free and instant.

[04:09.58 – 04:22.58]  Demo clip — mastery
Mastery per topic, the prerequisite graph coloured by what is known, and the weakest topic surfaced with a way straight into it.

[04:22.58 – 04:51.63]  Diagram — assessment
Assessment closes the loop. Questions are written from the learner's own material and tagged to a topic and a source location. Each one is then checked by a model from a different family than the one that wrote it, so the verification is genuinely independent rather than a model marking its own work. Duplicates are caught by fingerprint and by embedding similarity. Generation runs in the background, so you can leave the page and be told when the paper is ready.

[05:08.56 – 05:31.39]  Diagram — mindmap
Ingestion that keeps every source location. Retrieval that refuses when the evidence is thin. A learner model that moves with the student. Lucify ties those together so that studying is both traceable and personal — every answer can be opened at the page it came from, and nothing is invented.

[05:31.44 – 06:00.81]  Diagram — notes
Beyond the tutor, Lucify writes study material for you. Notes come in four formats — a summary, Cornell notes, flashcards, or question and answer — and the scope can be the whole course, one topic, or a single document. Whichever you choose, the notes are written only from retrieved passages, so each set ends with the sources it drew on, the key terms it found, and an honest note about anything your material does not cover.

[06:00.81 – 06:25.21]  Demo clip — notes demo
The format is chosen first, then the scope. This set was written earlier: the summary, the key equations it pulled out, and at the end the exact sources behind it, down to the page and the timestamp in the lecture. Generating a new one runs in the background, so you can leave the page while it writes.

[06:25.21 – 06:50.40]  Diagram — planner
The planner turns the learner model into a schedule. It takes the exam date and the minutes you can give per day, then weights the plan towards your weakest topics and folds in the spaced-repetition reviews that are already due. Sessions follow the prerequisite order the material implies, and a focus timer tracks what you actually finish, which feeds straight back into the model.

[06:50.40 – 07:04.30]  Demo clip — planner demo
Seventeen days to the exam, sixty minutes a day. The plan puts the weakest topics first, keeps prerequisites in order, and schedules the reviews that are due.

[07:04.30 – 07:36.00]  Diagram — memory
Lucify also builds a picture of how you learn. Preferences, goals and recurring confusions are picked up from what you type, from assessment results, and from feedback you give directly. This is done with rules rather than an extra model call, so it costs nothing to maintain. It is injected into the tutor prompt — and crucially, all of it is visible on one page, where you can edit, pin, archive or delete any entry. Nothing is remembered that you cannot see.

[07:36.00 – 07:53.37]  Demo clip — memory demo
Everything it remembers is listed in plain language, with where it came from. Each entry can be pinned, edited, archived or deleted. Insights then show mastery, the weakest topics, and what to study next.

[07:53.37 – 08:20.47]  Diagram — profiles
Finally, one key gives you profiles — separate workspaces, so coursework and side projects do not mix, each course staying its own knowledge base. Settings hold the theme, the optional Sarvam voice key, and the controls over that memory. That is Lucify: ingestion that keeps every source location, retrieval that refuses when the evidence is thin, and a learner model that keeps moving as you study.
